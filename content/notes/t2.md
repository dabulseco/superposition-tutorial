PAGE NOTES: Topic 2 — Coupled Oscillators

- System: wall - spring k - weight 1 - middle spring k_c - weight 2 -
  spring k - wall. Equal masses m. x1, x2 are displacements from rest
  (positive = right). The weights are released from rest.
- Two patterns (normal modes):
  - Pattern 1: both weights move together. omega_1 = sqrt(k/m). The middle
    spring is never stretched, so k_c doesn't matter for it.
  - Pattern 2: weights move opposite. omega_2 = sqrt((k + 2 k_c)/m). The
    middle spring is stretched twice as much, so it pulls harder: faster.
  - Defaults k = m = 1, k_c = 2.625 give omega_1 = 1.0 and omega_2 = 2.5.
- Any motion is a superposition of the two:
    x1(t) = A1 cos(omega_1 t) + A2 cos(omega_2 t)
    x2(t) = A1 cos(omega_1 t) - A2 cos(omega_2 t)
  with A1 = (x1_0 + x2_0)/2 and A2 = (x1_0 - x2_0)/2.
- Examples: (40, 40) gives A1 = 40, A2 = 0 (Pattern 1 only). (40, -40)
  gives A1 = 0, A2 = 40 (Pattern 2 only). (40, 0) gives A1 = A2 = 20 (equal
  amounts). (40, -10) gives A1 = 15, A2 = 25.
- The Fourier transform plot is computed from weight 1's simulated motion.
  It shows a peak at each pattern's frequency whose height is that
  pattern's amplitude. It is a measurement of the motion, not a drawing.
- Link to the other topics: the pair (A1, A2) is a two-part state, like a
  qubit's two amplitudes. The state card shows it as bars and as a point
  on a sphere. Unlike a qubit, this is classical: you can watch both
  patterns at once and nothing snaps when you look.
